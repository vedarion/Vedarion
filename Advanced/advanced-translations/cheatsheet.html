Advanced Translations

Tricky words, translated.

Not all & few
QUANTIFIERS
1
Not all / Not every = Some are not
At least one is not.
2
Few = Most are not
“Few vegetables are tasty” means most vegetables are not tasty. Few also implies some are, but the LSAT usually tests whether you know it means most are not.
Comparative words
COMPARATIVE DOESN’T MEAN ABSOLUTE
3
Taller than / more than
Relative only, not absolute. Example: “Rachel is taller than Sarah” doesn’t mean Rachel is tall or Sarah is short.
4
The biggest / the most
Only compared to the group, not absolute. Example: the biggest fish of the day can still be tiny.
INCLUDES THE EQUAL CASE
5
Not faster than = Same speed or slower
Includes the same speed. Example: Tom and Jerry could tie.
6
At least X = X or more
Exactly X counts.
7
At most X / No more than X = X or less
Anywhere from 0 up to X. Exactly X counts.
Or, not both, neither nor
TWO OPTIONS, A AND B
8
A or B = At least one
Could be both. If not A, then B. If not B, then A.
9
Not both A and B = At most one
Could be neither. If A, then not B. If B, then not A.
10
A or B, but not both = Exactly one
One is in, the other is out. All four translations above apply.
11
Neither A nor B = None
Not A and not B.
Biconditionals
ARROW GOES BOTH WAYS
12
If and only if / when and only when / if A, then B; otherwise, not B = A ↔ B
Both sufficient and necessary. The contrapositive goes both ways too.
Always ask: What exactly does this word allow?
