Complex Conditionals

Tricky conditionals, translated.

Unless / without / except / until
NEGATE THE SUFFICIENT
1
Unless / Without / Except / Until: identify the sufficient, then negate it
“A unless B”: A is sufficient, so negate it. /A → B
No / none / cannot
NEGATE THE NECESSARY
2
No / None / Cannot: identify the necessary, then negate it
“No A is B”: B is necessary, so negate it. A → /B
Cannot + unless / without
TWO NEGATIONS CANCEL
3
Cannot A without B = A → B
The two negation words cancel each other out. Read it as a straightforward conditional.
Advanced conditional theory
THE DEFINITION
4
If the sufficient happens, the necessary has to follow. Every time.
Before you draw an arrow, ask: if I know the first thing, does the second thing 100% follow? Yes: draw the arrow. No: no arrow, even if you see an indicator word.
HIDDEN CONDITIONAL (NO INDICATOR WORD)
5
“Fish live in water.” = Fish → Water
The sentence tells you that whenever something is a fish, it lives in water. So if you know it’s a fish, living in water 100% follows.
FAKE CONDITIONAL (INDICATOR WORD, NO GUARANTEE)
6
“Every fish can be orange.” = No arrow
“Can” only tells you a fish could be orange, so being orange doesn’t 100% follow. Watch for can, may, and might: they tell you something is possible, not guaranteed.
Always ask: Does the second thing 100% follow?
