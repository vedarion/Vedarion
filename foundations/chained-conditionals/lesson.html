Chained Conditionals

Linking A → B → C and drawing valid conclusions across the chain.

What you'll learn

Once you know how a single conditional works, the next step is connecting them. When the necessary condition of one statement is the sufficient condition of another, you can chain them together — and the LSAT loves testing whether you can follow that chain correctly.

  • How to link conditionals: if A → B and B → C, then A → C
  • Drawing valid conclusions across a chain
  • Contrapositives of chained conditionals
